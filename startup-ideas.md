# 15 Business Ideas, Researched and Stress-Tested

*September 26, 2026. Built for: 18-year-old Harvard freshman, goal of $1-5k/month side income in year one, 5-20 hrs/week, will do cold sales and deliver services, won't do on-camera content or run a community.*

## Read this first

**The headline.** Your rarest asset isn't React or the Harvard network. It's that you can do grant writing *and* financial modeling, a combination almost nobody your age has. All of the top five ideas draw on that background, and most need both halves of it, sold B2B to people who already pay for this work. Every consumer *app* either died in research or landed near the bottom, for one structural reason: without on-camera content or a community, you have no cheap way to reach consumers at scale. The consumer ideas that survived are services (Latin grading, tutoring, speech punch-ups), where you win one customer at a time. Cold email reaches businesses, not best men.

**The calibration you need.** Two skeptics independently estimated month-12 revenue for every idea. For the strongest ones, the median lands at **$600-1,500/month**, not $5k. $1k/month by next September is a realistic goal. $5k needs your best idea to work *and* a second one stacked on top. Nearly every winner is "service first, software later." That fits your constraints (you'll sell, and you get paid in weeks), but it means your hours are the ceiling until you productize.

**My recommendation.** Treat #1 and #3 as one business: a back office for freelance grant writers. Same buyer, same cold-email list, two products. Lead with the $249 Funder Audit (the fastest cash in the cluster), upsell the budget desk ($250-750 per job), and keep #2 BoardPacket as the year-two move into recurring revenue. If you need money before that ramps, #7 Latin tutoring is the fastest cash on the list, but only if your Latin is genuinely advanced. For the game slot, #15 Gambit Run deserves a time-boxed shot over winter break. #11 is really a slow content business wearing a game costume.

**Decide in 3 weeks, not 3 months.** Run the three cheapest demand tests in parallel:
1. 50 cold emails to freelance grant writers offering the budget desk plus a white-label funder audit (#1 and #3).
2. 50 emails to education foundations with 3 free, vetted funder matches and a $249 audit offer (#3).
3. 20 emails to business brokers with a free bankability screen of one of their BizBuySell listings (#4).

Whichever gets 2+ *paid* orders first gets your semester. Everything else waits.

**Four things that apply to almost every idea.**
- **Taxes and aid.** Once you net $400+ a year, you owe self-employment tax (about 15.3%) plus income tax, so set aside 25-30% of profit and track expenses. If you're on financial aid, business income counts as student income on next year's FAFSA and CSS Profile, so check with the aid office before it gets meaningful. (The F-1 caveats inside individual entries don't apply to you as a citizen.)
- **Harvard's name.** "A Harvard student" as a bio line is fine. Harvard in a brand name, logo or domain is not. Don't pitch through House or club email lists without permission either.
- **Legal hygiene eats the budget.** The B2B finance ideas want an LLC (Massachusetts charges $500/yr), an engagement letter capping your liability at the fee, and eventually E&O insurance ($500-1,500/yr). That's most of $1,500. Start with the engagement letter and add the rest once revenue justifies it.
- **The calendar.** Grant deadlines, SBA closings, NLE season and HOA budget season don't pause for finals. Every service here needs a published turnaround time and blackout weeks from day one.

## The 15 ideas

### 1. Grant Budget Desk: the white-label budget person for freelance grant writers

**What it is.** A service first, software second. Freelance grant writers send you the RFP, staff list and costs. You return a reconciled budget, a justification tied line by line to the numbers, the SF-424A or funder format, and a one-page reconciliation report proving every number ties out. The MVP is a private Google Sheets or React engine you build while delivering: staff split across funders, fringe, 15% de minimis vs negotiated indirect, escalation, and a numbers-to-text checker. No public SaaS until 20-30 paid jobs.

**Who pays.** Freelance grant writers and boutique grant firms who hate budgets and can mark your work up: an estimated 10k-30k in the US (GPA has 4,000+ members, AGWA about 1,200). Secondary: EDs at some of the 50k-100k nonprofits in the $250k-$5M band juggling 4+ funders. Skip university research offices; Cayuse and Kuali own them.

**How much.** $250-400 foundation budget, $450-750 federal/state package, $1,500-2,000 multi-funder cost-allocation workbook. Math: 3 packages x ~$375 = ~$1.1k, plus one workbook a quarter (~$500/mo) = ~$1.6k in a good month. Realistic median at month 12: $600-1,800/mo, landing near $1k. The original $29/mo SaaS plan tops out around $300-900 MRR.

**Why it's not already solved.** Automate Impact sells nearly this exact outcome as a $3,000 custom build, out of reach for freelancers. FundRobin and SciSpace give away AI justification prose with no math behind it. Instrumentl ($299+/mo) users "still do the heavy lifting on every budget justification." Grantable and ChatGPT draft narrative that can be "quietly wrong" on MTDC and escalation. Etsy templates ($5-30) reconcile nothing.

**Your unfair advantage.** Real and rare: grant writing plus auditable financial modeling. The deterministic engine is what a model wrapper can't fake. Lead with that background, never your age.

**Brutal tradeoffs.**
- Demand is inferred, not observed. Nobody on forums is asking for this.
- The highest-value use case is shrinking: 21% of nonprofits lost some government funding in 2025. Foundation budgets are simpler, and Excel plus ChatGPT often suffices.
- Grant deadlines ignore finals. Promise 5 business days and publish a blackout/rush-fee policy.
- Liability: a wrong MTDC base can cost a client an award. Use an agreement capping liability at the fee, with the client owning allowability and submission. Flat fees only (GPA ethics bans percentage-of-award pay). If you're on an F-1 visa, this is unauthorized work.
- Keep client salary data out of consumer AI tools.

**First 30 days.**
1. Send 30-50 personalized cold emails a week to GPA/AGWA members, Upwork freelancers and boutique firms: "I take the budget section off your plate; you keep the client." Goal: one paid $250-450 job by day 30.
2. Build a mock federal budget with a reconciliation report as your portfolio sample, and draft the services agreement.
3. Deliver first jobs in Google Sheets, adding allocation, indirect and checker logic only as real jobs demand it.

**Scorecard:** Speed to cash 3/5 | Edge 5/5 | Ceiling 2/5 | Risk Med

**Key sources:** [Automate Impact Budget Creator ($3k)](https://www.automateimpact.org/tools/budget-creator) · [FundRobin free justification tool](https://fundrobin.com/free-tools/budget-justification) · [Urban Institute on 2025 funding disruptions](https://www.urban.org/research/publication/how-government-funding-disruptions-affected-nonprofits-early-2025) · [Freelance grant writer fees](https://i-grants.com/blogs/freelance-grant-writing-fees)

### 2. BoardPacket: white-label grant spend-down and board packets for QuickBooks nonprofits

**What it is.** A back-office service, not SaaS (yet). A nonprofit bookkeeper or ED sends standard QBO exports (Budget vs Actual by Class, P&L by Class, Balance Sheet) plus a one-time grant register. Within 3 business days you return a 4-6 page PDF: grant spend-down pacing, upcoming funder report deadlines, functional expense split, cash runway and a draft narrative. The month-1 MVP is a Sheets template plus a script that pulls 990 e-file data for a peer page. Later comes a React tool that turns exports into the PDF, used internally only.

**Who pays.** The main buyer is nonprofit-specialist bookkeepers and fractional CFOs (an estimated few thousand US firms), who buy wholesale and put their own brand on it. Second, you can sell direct to QBO nonprofits with $750k-$5M in revenue and 3+ restricted grants. That's roughly 8k-15k strong-fit orgs: 40k-60k orgs in the $300k-$5M band, times a 35-55% QBO share, times about half holding meaningful restricted grants.

**How much.** Wholesale: $300-500 onboarding per org, then $75-125/org/mo, with a 5-org minimum. Direct: $1,500 onboarding, then $300-450/mo billed quarterly. Math: 2 firms x 5 orgs x $100 = $1k, plus 3 direct orgs x $300 = $900, gets you about $1.9k/mo. The realistic median at month 12 is $600-2,000/mo, plus lumpy onboarding fees.

**Why it's not already solved.** GrantLink (pricing not public) and Actually (early beta) already sell QBO grant spend-down software and are doing SEO on your exact keywords, so skip the SaaS race. Reach Reporting ($149/mo, or $290 for 10 connections) builds board reports, but you have to set up the grant logic yourself, and it has no 990 benchmarks. Aplos ($79-229/mo, with budget by fund only at $229) means migrating off QBO. Bookkeepers charge $400-3,500/mo and often just hand the board a raw QBO P&L. Nobody sells the finished packet at small-org prices.

**Your unfair advantage.** You actually understand restricted vs. unrestricted money and how funders want spend-down reported, and your comps background makes the 990 peer page sharp. The edge on substance is real. The edge on credibility is weak, which is why the white-label channel matters: the bookkeeper holds the client's trust.

**Brutal tradeoffs.**
- Packets are due on fixed dates 1-2 weeks after month-end close, and some of those dates will land on midterms and finals. Cap yourself at 12-15 orgs and write service-level deadlines that fit your calendar.
- The real work is often cleaning up messy books, not building the packet. Put cleanup in the onboarding scope or you'll drown.
- There's a cheap substitute: a bookkeeper pastes an export into Claude and gets 80% of the way in 20 minutes. What you're selling is grant logic and reliability.
- Legal: never call it audited or reviewed statements, never imply CPA work, and don't prepare 990s. Use an engagement letter, NDAs and E&O insurance (about $500-1,000/yr). Anything that touches the QBO API triggers Intuit's security assessment (6 weeks to 6+ months), so stay on exports.
- Finance committees slow down purchases, so $1k MRR likely takes 6-9 months.

**First 30 days.**
1. Find nonprofit bookkeepers in the QuickBooks ProAdvisor directory and email 50 of them. Include a free 990 peer-ratio page on one of their clients plus a sample packet, and offer a paid 3-org pilot at wholesale rates.
2. Interview 10 bookkeepers. Do they see you as a partner or a threat? How often do their clients' boards meet? If it's quarterly, bill quarterly.
3. Form an LLC and draft the engagement letter. Deliver the first 2-3 packets by hand in Sheets and time each one.

**Scorecard:** Speed to cash 2/5 | Edge 4/5 | Ceiling 4/5 | Risk Med

**Key sources:** [Reach Reporting pricing](https://reachreporting.com/pricing) | [GrantLink](https://grantlink.app/) | [Nonprofit bookkeeping costs 2026](https://www.remotebooksonline.com/blog/nonprofit-bookkeeping-cost) | [Intuit App Partner Program pricing](https://www.apideck.com/blog/quickbooks-api-pricing-and-the-intuit-app-partner-program)

### 3. Funder Match: vetted "who funds orgs like mine" audits for school foundations

**What it is.** A $249 Funder Audit for one vertical, K-12 education foundations and charter/classical school foundations. The client names 3-5 peer orgs. Within 5-7 business days you deliver 15-20 hand-vetted foundations pulled from those peers' 990-PF grant histories, each labeled open/LOI/invite-only, with typical grant size relative to the client's budget, a contact or approach route, and a 12-month calendar. The MVP is a spreadsheet template plus ProPublica and Grantmakers.io lookups, sold manually in weeks 1-3. Your only code is an internal 990-PF XML parser that gets vetting under an hour.

**Who pays.** Executive directors of education nonprofits with $150k-$2M budgets and no development staff. There are about 6k local education foundations plus about 7.8k charter schools. Freelance grant writers are a second buyer (an estimated 5k-15k in the US), buying white-label research to bill to their clients.

**How much.** $249 per audit. On top of that, either a $39/mo "Peer Watch" refresh or a $450-750/mo "Grant Desk" retainer (quarterly refresh plus one LOI drafted per month), and $79-149 white-label reports for consultants. Example math: 3 audits ($750) + 1-2 retainers ($500-1,000) + a few wholesale reports ($100-250) = ~$1.3-2k in a good month. Realistic median at month 12 is **$600-2,000/mo, centered around $1-1.2k**.

**Why it's not already solved.** Instrumentl puts peer prospecting in its $499/mo tier, which small orgs call budget-breaking. Candid is free or cheap now (free tier, Gold Seal free year, library access), but it's a self-serve tool with a steep learning curve and no vetting. Grantable does AI 990 matching for about $75/mo discounted, with no human check on invite-only or geography flags. Fiverr lists cost $10-60 and are mostly unvetted RFP scraping. The data is free. What nobody sells one-time is judgment.

**Your unfair advantage.** Real grant-prospecting experience means you know what "fit" means in practice. Equity research taught you to turn filings into a thesis. Both critics rate your founder fit 8/10. The edge is thin, though: it's skill, not data, and AI is closing the gap every quarter.

**Brutal tradeoffs.**
- It's mostly a service capped by your hours, and one-time reports mean you start every month at zero with cold email.
- Small-org EDs are cash-poor, overloaded, and wary of an 18-year-old vendor. Lead with the work, not the bio.
- Funder deadlines don't move for your finals. Missing one is reputationally fatal, so only sell LOI drafting in windows you control.
- Some states (NY, CA, MA and others) require fundraising-counsel registration, so check before selling retainers there. Charge flat fees only (never a commission), and never build reports from a library or Harvard Candid login.
- 990 data is 12-24 months stale, and one bad match hurts credibility.

**First 30 days.**
1. Pull 100 education foundations from IRS/990 data. Send each a cold email with 3 real, vetted peer-funder matches and a $249 audit offer with a money-back guarantee if fewer than 8-10 funders are actionable. Aim for 5 paid audits, not free ones.
2. Talk to 20 freelance grant writers about white-label reports at $79-149.
3. Deliver the audits by hand, time each step, and only then build the 990-PF parser for the slowest step.

**Scorecard:** Speed to cash 4/5 | Edge 4/5 | Ceiling 2/5 | Risk Med

**Key sources:** [Grantmakers.io (free peer lookup)](https://www.grantmakers.io/) · [Candid free access changes](https://candid.org/blogs/search-nonprofits-candid-free-access/) · [Grantable pricing](https://grantable.co/pricing) · [Instrumentl pricing for small orgs](https://www.fundrobin.com/articles/how-to-guide/ai-tools-for-nonprofits/instrumentl-pricing-roi-small-nonprofits-2026/)

### 4. Bankable: white-label SDE recasts and SBA bankability screens for business brokers

**What it is.** A flat-fee back office for licensed business brokers. The broker sends the seller's 3 years of returns and P&Ls. In 3-5 business days you send back a workbook plus a 2-3 page memo in the broker's branding: a return-to-SDE reconciliation, each add-back rated green/yellow/red for lender risk, SBA 7(a) debt service including the guarantee fee, the max supportable price at 1.25x and 1.50x DSCR, and a seller-note structure. MVP in 3-4 weeks: Excel template, secure upload, memo template.

**Who pays.** Main Street brokers and small M&A advisors: roughly 4,000-8,000 active in the US (dossier estimate), and maybe 300-800 of them would pay for a human-run service. Each closes only 3-4 deals a year (IBBA Market Pulse 2025).

**How much.** $350 per listing, $250 each in a 4-pack, +$150 for rush. Upsell: once a listing is under LOI, a full SBA loan package (projections, global cash flow, sources and uses) at $600-800, billed to the broker. Math: 4 brokers x 1/mo x $350 = ~$1.4k/mo. $3k needs ~10 jobs (~60 hours) a month. Realistic month-12 median: $600-1,500/mo, near $1k.

**Why it's not already solved.** BizBen sells AI CIMs at $14-40, and Vertica CRM says its generator does no financial analysis. ProjectionHub charges $750 for SBA projections but sells straight to borrowers and has no white-label product for brokers. Fiverr SBA plans ($15-295) are weak on DSCR.

**Your unfair advantage.** Normalizing financials and stress-testing coverage is equity research, and the engine can become a $49-99/mo React tool later. But the edge is thin: brokers learn recasting in CBI training, Schedule C cash businesses aren't GAAP filers, and ex-SBA underwriters on Upwork have credibility you don't.

**Brutal tradeoffs.**
- Older brokers have to send an 18-year-old tax returns full of SSNs. Expect slow sales cycles.
- It only grows with your hours: 4-8 per recast early on, 12-20 per full package. Sell listing-stage work, not LOI deadlines, and publish finals blackout weeks.
- Legal: bill brokers only, flat fee, no buyer contact or success fees (about 15 states license broker activity, and under SBA Form 159 you become a disclosed Agent if borrowers pay you). Label output "not an appraisal, not audited," never call it "QoE," and tie every number to the returns (18 U.S.C. 1014 exposure).
- E&O (~$500-1,500/yr) and a MA LLC ($500/yr) can eat the budget. Start with an engagement letter capping liability at fees paid.

**First 30 days.**
1. Screen 50 BizBuySell listings using public data (asking price and stated SDE versus what a lender would support) and email each broker: "Send the returns and I'll do the real one."
2. Get 10 broker calls. Kill it if fewer than 3 would pay $250+, or if you don't have 2 paid screens by early December.
3. Build only what paid jobs need: workbook, NDA, engagement letter, encrypted upload.

**Scorecard:** Speed to cash 3/5 | Edge 3/5 | Ceiling 3/5 | Risk Med

**Key sources:** [Vertica: AI CIM does no financial analysis](https://businessbrokerscrm.com/ai-confidential-information-memorandum-cim-generator-for-business-brokers-vertica-crms-native-true-context-ai/) | [BizBen AI CIM pricing](https://www.bizben.com/buy/cim-generations) | [ProjectionHub pricing](https://www.projectionhub.com/pricing) | [IBBA Market Pulse Q3 2025](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/the-ibba-and-ma-source-announce-the-results-of-the-market-pulse-q3-2025-survey-302617915.html)

### 5. SBIR Market Model Desk: sourced bottom-up market models for federal deep-tech grant proposals

**What it is.** A flat-fee "Sourced Market Model + Commercialization Appendix" for SBIR/STTR proposals: a bottom-up spreadsheet (named segments x validated price x close rate, every number sourced) plus the narrative in the agency's review format. Sell it white-label to independent SBIR consultants who fold it into their $5-7k packages, and direct to NSF software, hardware and climate founders. The MVP is templates, not software. Later, add real customer interviews and letters of support, the part AI deep research can't fake.

**Who pays.** SBIR consultants and 1-10 person spinouts. About 3,200 Phase I and 1,900 Phase II awards a year implies 15,000-25,000 Phase I proposals and an estimated 2,500-5,000 paid outside engagements. You need 1-2 a month.

**How much.** $300 review; $600-1,000 white-label model; $1,200 direct NSF Phase I section; $2,000-3,000 Phase II evidence pack after 5+ delivered models. Math: 1 model x $900 + a $300 review every other month = ~$1,050/mo. Realistic month-12 median: $800-2,000/mo (~$1.1-1.4k), very lumpy.

**Why it's not already solved.** SBIR Grant Writers sells a $5,995 full NSF Phase I; FundUnlocked covers only the Project Pitch ($349-599); ScienceDocs bills $170-180/hr for editing, not modeling; FreeMind wants ~$60k/yr. Nobody prices the market section alone. Granted AI ($29-89/mo) and $175 templates produce generic text, and reviewers call generic market claims the #1 failure reason. Caveat: the missing SKU may mean buyers want one vendor.

**Your unfair advantage.** Equity-research modeling plus grant-writing format is a rare combo, white-labeling hides your age, and Kendall spinouts are walkable. But the edge is thinner than it looks: no SBIR track record, no FDA/reimbursement or DoD acquisition depth, and AI is commoditizing desk-research TAM.

**Brutal tradeoffs.**
- Credibility is everything: a failed proposal costs founders 6-12 months and, from FY2027, a capped proposal slot.
- 20-35 hours per Phase I model, 40-70 for an NIH Phase II plan, so $45-75/hr effective. Consulting labor, hard to productize.
- NIH's Jan 5 and Apr 5 deadlines land on finals and midterms. Skip Jan 5 in year 1.
- Legal: no success fees (FAR 31.205-33(f)); TABA can't pay for proposal writing; NDAs required; skip DoD/ITAR topics; client signs off on every figure (False Claims Act exposure). If you're on an F-1 visa, off-campus freelancing likely isn't allowed.

**First 30 days.**
1. Build one sample model for a public NSF awardee, then cold-message 30 SBIR consultants on Upwork/LinkedIn offering a $600 white-label job. Goal: one paid order.
2. Offer 2-3 Boston spinouts (via i-lab, Engine, Greentown managers; ask about vendor rules first) a $400-600 pilot for a named case study.
3. Recruit a senior co-reviewer (former NSF SBIR awardee or reviewer) on a 20-25% revenue share; form an LLC with a simple NDA/MSA.

**Scorecard:** Speed to cash 3/5 | Edge 3/5 | Ceiling 2/5 | Risk Med

**Key sources:** [SBIR writing services compared, 2026](https://sbirgrantwriters.com/sbir-blog-grant-writing-services-compared-2026) | [FundUnlocked pricing roundup](https://fundunlocked.com/best-sbir-grant-writing-services-2026/) | [Cada commercialization plan guide](https://www.getcada.com/insights/sbir-commercialization-plan-guide) | [NIH TABA notice NOT-OD-26-075](https://grants.nih.gov/grants/guide/notice-files/NOT-OD-26-075.html)

### 6. Latin Homework Check: you grade the translations homeschool parents can't

**What it is.** Weekly grading for homeschool families using Memoria Press Second-Fourth Form and Henle I, where one printed answer key stops being enough. Parents upload the week's pages; within 48-72 hours you return marked corrections, accepted alternative translations, error explanations in the book's own terms, and a short lesson-limited quiz. You draft with an LLM plus a morphology tool (Whitaker's Words or Collatinus) and review everything. Later, productize it into a self-serve checker for one book by August 2027.

**Who pays.** Parents teaching Latin they never learned. The dossier estimates 50k-120k US families actively teaching a Latin curriculum (from ~3.4M homeschool students); First Form alone is maybe 20k-40k, upper books fewer. Also: co-op tutors and hybrid schools.

**How much.** Concierge $29/mo per student (or $199 prepaid for the school year), capped at 20-30 students. Later: self-serve $89-119/yr, tutor seats $199-399/yr. Math: 15 students x $29 = ~$435/mo, plus 60 families x $99/yr = ~$500/mo amortized. Realistic median at month 12: $300-1,000/mo, with a summer trough near $200-400.

**Why it's not already solved.** Publisher keys (First Form Complete Set $136.89) give one answer, no explanations. MP streaming (~$125) and CAP Online Practice ($23.95) are drill, not grading. Classical Quest ($95-144/yr) is drill plus a grammar tutor, not translation grading. Human grading (Homeschool Connections Instructor Access, Memoria Academy at $645/yr) costs far more. ChatGPT is free but drifts outside lesson scope and makes confident form errors parents can't catch.

**Your unfair advantage.** You know Latin, so you catch what parents can't, and you'll cold-sell and deliver by hand. Real but narrow: the software moat over frontier chatbots is thin.

**Brutal tradeoffs.**
- Small market, and free AI is "good enough" for many. If chatbots score under 2% error on your benchmark, stay a premium human service.
- Turnaround collides with midterms and finals; publish SLAs that pause during finals weeks.
- Only two real selling windows (January, August-September 2027), through gatekeepers; CC is moving to its own Common Latin in 2027-28.
- Legal: never store or republish publisher keys, exercises, or chant text; say "works with First Form Latin" with a non-affiliation disclaimer; parent-only accounts and minimal retention of kids' handwriting photos (COPPA).
- Classical Quest or Memoria Press could ship this as a feature.

**First 30 days.**
1. This week, cold-email 50 independent (non-CC) co-ops and MP-using hybrid schools offering $29/mo or $199 prepaid. Goal: 5 paying families by day 30.
2. Simultaneously run the 100-sentence benchmark (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) on Second/Third Form and Henle sentences.
3. Build only an internal grading tool to cut per-page time; send Memoria Press a courtesy email.

**Scorecard:** Speed to cash 4/5 | Edge 3/5 | Ceiling 2/5 | Risk Med

**What it is.** A web-first roguelite where every encounter is a real rated tactic from Lichess's CC0 database (6.1M theme-tagged puzzles). Relics change which puzzles you draw and how they score, not the moves: a pin floor for 2x gold, motif streak multipliers, risk puzzles 300 points above your rating, HP lost on mistakes. MVP in 8-10 weeks: a free daily seeded web run with a share card (no global leaderboard; engines make it cheatable) plus a $4.99 web unlock.

**Who pays.** Chess players who like games. Chess.com's 250M members are top of funnel only; critics put real buyers for a newcomer in the low thousands, lifetime. Side door: independent coaches and adult clubs at $99/yr (thousands of programs; ChessKid owns kids).

**How much.** $4.99 unlock via Lemon Squeezy, $99-149/yr coach license, $7.99-9.99 on Steam later. Good month: 3k players x 1.5% x $4.99 = ~$225, plus 30 coach licenses = ~$300/mo. $1k/mo needs ~150-170 Steam sales monthly, sustained. Realistic month-12 median: **$0-300/mo**, with a 10-15% shot at $1k+ only via a Silver-tier Steam launch (8k wishlists).

**Why it's not already solved.** Gambonanza (May 2026, 200k+ units in two weeks) and Shotgun King (~$1.86M gross) prove the genre, but they, Pawnbarian and The Ouroboros King all bend chess rules. Lichess Storm is free and Puzzle Rush sits inside Chess.com's ~$5-15/mo membership; neither has runs or builds. Caveat: the slot may be empty because fixed puzzles make shallow builds.

**Your unfair advantage.** Real design edge: you know which motifs feel great, and React plus chess.js is your stack. Your sales skills only help the coach tier.

**Brutal tradeoffs.**
- Lottery ticket. Gambonanza's dev sold under 700 units across two prior games; 11 chess games sold for $12 in a Humble bundle.
- It still earned the game slot: cheapest test here, no clients, survives finals, sellable asset.
- Revenue spikes then decays, and a real Steam launch likely lands mid-to-late 2027.
- Legal: no GPL Chessground/Stockfish in closed builds; no Lichess or Harvard branding; COPPA-safe teacher-managed accounts; no visible AI art.

**First 30 days.**
1. Prototype the relic economy in 2 weeks; playtest with ~30 chess club and House players. Gate: median session 15+ min and unprompted second runs.
2. Make the $4.99 unlock a live preorder the day the slice goes public, so demand shows up in dollars.
3. Cold email 20 coaches offering a $99 founding license. Cap total effort at ~60 hours; kill at end of winter break unless D7 retention hits 10%+, and open a Steam page only at 1k+ daily players.

**Scorecard:** Speed to cash 1/5 | Edge 3/5 | Ceiling 4/5 | Risk High

## What didn't make the cut

**Survived research but ranked below the line**
- **Yes, And (pass-the-phone improv party game).** Your improv edge is real, but hitting $1k/month takes 9-12k downloads a month, and that volume usually comes from on-camera TikTok challenges, which you've ruled out.
- **Portfolio (personal-finance sim for high school classes).** Free, sponsor-funded incumbents (NGPF, Banzai, Budget Challenge) own the category and the teacher channel.
- **ESA vendor approvals.** Approval is free and quick, and the leading tool publicly warns vendors away from paid application services.
- **DECA/FBLA role-play trainer.** At least 8 AI judges already exist, several free, and DECA+ owns the chapter budget line.

**Killed by both skeptics**
- **LD debate framework briefs.** 2-5k buyers split across 6+ sellers, who buy on a debate pedigree you don't have.
- **LabRunway (grant forecasting for research labs).** No evidence anyone pays, university procurement blocks the buyer, and free institutional tools exist.

**Cut before research (a few notable ones)**
- **Endowment second opinions for small nonprofits.** Getting paid to evaluate investment advisers likely requires state RIA registration, including in MA.
- **SEC filing diffs, small-cap IR peer digests, cited SEC data in Sheets.** AlphaSense, Koyfin, Bloomberg AI and brokerages are bundling all of it.
- **Nonprofit formation and compliance autopilot.** Real unauthorized-practice-of-law exposure.
- **Daily deduction puzzle, Latin grammar games for teachers.** The first needs 30-50k monthly players in a format Murdle and NYT Games can own. The second is too niche (about 3-4k Latin teachers).

## How this list was made

- **Generate:** 8 brainstorming agents, each with its own lens (nonprofits and grants, finance, small-business B2B, education, your passions, games, micro-SaaS, AI-native), produced 64 ideas. A completeness critic added 6 more from angles they'd missed.
- **Shortlist:** an editor merged duplicates and cut 24 ideas that failed a criterion or one of your constraints, leaving 22 (4 of them games).
- **Research and attack:** each of the 22 got live web research on competitors, pricing and market size (all 22 web-verified). Then two independent skeptics tried to kill it: one on demand and competition, one on whether *you specifically* can win it.
- **Judge:** three judges ranked all 22 on speed to cash, founder edge, and long-term upside. Rankings were combined by Borda count, and the top 13 non-games plus the top 2 games made the list. Two ideas that converged on the same buyer (SDE recasts and SBA packages, both for business brokers) were merged into #4.
- **Write:** each finalist was written up from its research dossier and critiques, then spot-checked on review. About 95 agent runs in total.

Treat prices and market sizes as good-faith research estimates, not audited figures. Verify the one you pick before you bet a semester on it.
